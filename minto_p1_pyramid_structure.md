# Minto Pyramid Principle - Logic in Writing

## Part 1: Why a Pyramid Structure (Communication Fundamentals)

### Core Concept

Clear financial communication requires a pyramid structure because the human mind automatically groups information into logical units and processes it top-down. When presenting valuation analyses, investment recommendations, or financial reports, readers comprehend ideas faster when they receive the main conclusion first, followed by supporting arguments in logical groupings. This structure mirrors how the mind naturally organizes information and answers the reader's questions as they arise.

### The Minto Framework: Building Pyramids for Financial Writing

#### Principle 1: The Mind Sorts Information into Pyramids

**Why this matters in finance**:

When an investment committee reads your recommendation to "Acquire Company X for $500M," their minds immediately begin organizing the supporting facts you present. If you provide those facts randomly (revenue growth, then EBITDA margin, then market share, then debt levels), the reader must do the mental work of grouping them into logical categories (financial performance, market position, capital structure). This cognitive burden reduces clarity and persuasiveness.

**The Solution**: Pre-organize your thinking into the pyramid structure the reader's mind naturally creates.

**Structure**:
```
Main Point (Pyramid Top)
    ↓
Supporting Arguments (3-5 key reasons)
    ↓ ↓ ↓
Evidence/Data (facts supporting each reason)
```

**Financial Example - Before (Data Dump)**:
```
Revenue grew 15% last year. The company has 22% market share. 
EBITDA margin expanded 200bps to 24%. Industry multiples are 12x 
EV/EBITDA. Net debt is $300M. Working capital improved $50M. 
The management team has 15 years experience. Competitors are 
trading at 14x. Therefore, we should buy the stock.
```

**After (Pyramid Structure)**:
```
RECOMMENDATION: Buy XYZ stock (Target: $45, 35% upside)

Why?
1. Strong financial momentum supports valuation expansion
   • Revenue CAGR 15% (vs. sector 10%)
   • EBITDA margin expansion 200bps to 24%
   • Working capital efficiency improving

2. Undervalued vs. comparable companies  
   • Trading at 12x EV/EBITDA (peers: 14x)
   • Quality justifies premium, not discount
   
3. Experienced management de-risks execution
   • Team averaged 15+ years industry experience
   • Track record of successful integrations
```

#### Principle 2: The Magical Number Seven

**The cognitive constraint**: Research shows the mind can hold approximately 7 ± 2 items in short-term memory at once. Beyond this, comprehension drops significantly.

**Application to financial writing**:

**❌ Poor Structure (12 reasons to invest)**:
```
We recommend investing because of:
1. Revenue growth
2. Margin expansion  
3. Market share gains
4. New product pipeline
5. Cost synergies
6. Management quality
7. Balance sheet strength
8. Industry tailwinds
9. Regulatory changes
10. Technology leadership
11. Customer retention
12. Geographic expansion
```

Reader reaction: *Overwhelming. Which reasons actually matter?*

**✅ Pyramid Structure (3 grouped reasons)**:
```
We recommend investing for three reasons:

1. Strong financial performance (items 1,2,3,6,7)
   • Revenue/margin/market share trends
   • Quality management execution
   
2. Competitive advantages (items 4,10,11)
   • Product innovation pipeline
   • Technology moat and customer stickiness
   
3. Favorable external environment (items 5,8,9,12)
   • Industry growth + regulatory tailwinds
   • Geographic expansion opportunities
```

**Rule**: Group ideas into 3-5 main points at each pyramid level. If you have more, create logical groupings.

#### Principle 3: Vertical Relationships (Question/Answer Logic)

**The Rule**: Each level of the pyramid answers a question triggered by the statement above it.

**Financial Application**:

```
Statement: "Divest the European division"
    ↓ (Reader asks: Why?)
    
Supporting Point: "It destroys shareholder value"
    ↓ (Reader asks: How do you know?)
    
Evidence:
    • ROIC 4% < WACC 8% (value destruction)
    • 5-year cumulative economic profit: -$200M
    • Market share declining 5% annually
    • No credible turnaround plan
```

**Testing vertical logic**:

After writing each statement, ask: "What question would a skeptical CFO ask?" 

The next level down must answer that exact question.

**Common Mistake**:
```
Statement: "Acquire Company X"
    ↓
Support: "The company has good products"
```

This fails because "good products" doesn't answer "Why should we acquire them?" 

The question is about value creation, not product quality.

**Fixed**:
```
Statement: "Acquire Company X"
    ↓ (Why?)
Support: "Creates $150M in shareholder value (25% IRR)"
    ↓ (How?)
Evidence: 
    • Revenue synergies: $50M (cross-selling)
    • Cost synergies: $30M (duplicate functions)
    • Multiple arbitrage: $70M (buy at 8x, peers at 12x)
```

#### Principle 4: Horizontal Relationships (Logical Grouping)

Ideas at the same level must be related by the same kind of logic. Two types:

**Type 1: Deductive Reasoning (Argument)**

Follows "if-then" logic. Used for proving a point.

```
Structure:
Major premise: [General rule]
Minor premise: [Specific case]
Conclusion: [Logical result]
```

**Financial Example (Credit Analysis)**:
```
Major premise: Companies with Debt/EBITDA > 6x are high credit risk
Minor premise: This company's Debt/EBITDA is 7.2x
Conclusion: Therefore, this is a high credit risk (don't lend)
```

**When to use in finance**:
- Audit findings (if standard violated → must remediate)
- Compliance issues (if covenant breached → technical default)
- Risk assessments (if metric exceeds threshold → downgrade)

**Type 2: Inductive Reasoning (Grouping)**

Groups similar items and draws a conclusion from the pattern.

```
Structure:
Observation 1: [Similar item]
Observation 2: [Similar item]  
Observation 3: [Similar item]
Conclusion: [Summary of similarity]
```

**Financial Example (Investment Thesis)**:
```
Observation 1: ROIC 18% exceeds WACC 9% by 900bps
Observation 2: Revenue CAGR 25% (sector: 12%)
Observation 3: Net promoter score 70 (industry leader)

Conclusion: Durable competitive advantages justify premium valuation
```

**When to use in finance**:
- Investment theses (multiple reasons support buy/sell)
- Strategic recommendations (several benefits of action)
- Problem diagnoses (various symptoms point to root cause)

**Critical Rule**: Ideas grouped inductively must be the **same kind of idea**.

**❌ Mixed logic (breaks horizontal relationship)**:
```
We should divest because:
1. ROIC < WACC (financial performance)
2. Management wants to focus elsewhere (strategic intent)
3. Buyer offering 12x EBITDA (transaction opportunity)
```

These are different kinds of reasons (performance, strategy, valuation), making the grouping illogical.

**✅ Consistent logic**:
```
Divest for three financial reasons:
1. Returns below cost of capital (ROIC 4% < WACC 8%)
2. Negative economic profit trend (-$50M annually)
3. No path to acceptable returns (even optimistic case: ROIC 6%)
```

All three are financial performance reasons (same kind of idea).

### The SCQA Introduction Framework

**For every financial document, structure the introduction as**: Situation-Complication-Question-Answer

#### Component 1: Situation (Set the Context)

Establishes the stable, agreed-upon facts. No controversy.

**Investment Memo Example**:
```
Situation: "XYZ Corp is a $2B revenue enterprise software company 
focused on CRM solutions for mid-market businesses. The company 
went public in 2020 and currently trades at $35/share (8x EV/Revenue)."
```

**Board Paper Example**:
```
Situation: "Our European division generates €500M revenue with 
12% EBITDA margins, employing 2,000 people across 15 countries."
```

**Rule**: Start with facts the reader already knows or can easily verify.

#### Component 2: Complication (Create Tension)

Introduces a problem, opportunity, or change that disrupts the situation.

**Investment Memo**:
```
Complication: "Despite 30% revenue growth and 85% gross margins, 
the stock has underperformed the software sector by 40% over 
12 months, with no clear catalyst for re-rating."
```

**Board Paper**:
```
Complication: "A strategic buyer has offered to acquire the division 
for €800M (12x EBITDA), 50% above current carrying value but 
below our long-term value projections."
```

**Rule**: The complication should trigger a question in the reader's mind.

#### Component 3: Question (Reader's Implied Question)

Makes explicit the question arising from the complication.

**Investment Memo**:
```
Question: "Is this underperformance justified by fundamental 
weaknesses, or does it represent a compelling buying opportunity?"
```

**Board Paper**:
```
Question: "Should we accept the offer and realize immediate value, 
or retain the division for long-term value creation?"
```

**Rule**: This must be a question the reader actually cares about (not a question you want to answer).

#### Component 4: Answer (Your Recommendation/Conclusion)

Your main point - the top of the pyramid.

**Investment Memo**:
```
Answer: "Strong Buy with $55 price target (57% upside). Market 
is mispricing durable competitive advantages and underestimating 
margin expansion potential."
```

**Board Paper**:
```
Answer: "Recommend accepting the offer. Immediate value realization 
outweighs uncertain long-term upside given execution risks and 
capital allocation alternatives."
```

**Rule**: This becomes the top of your pyramid. Everything below supports this answer.

### Complete SCQA Example: Private Equity Investment Memo

**Situation**:
```
TechCo is a B2B SaaS company with $150M ARR, growing 40% annually 
in the marketing automation space. The company is founder-owned, 
profitable (15% EBITDA margin), and seeking its first institutional 
capital raise.
```

**Complication**:
```
The founder is willing to sell a minority stake (40%) but insists on 
retaining operational control and maintaining current growth investments, 
which depress near-term margins. This structure limits our governance 
rights while requiring a $200M equity check at 20x EBITDA—above our 
typical 12-15x entry range for minority deals.
```

**Question**:
```
Does the premium valuation and minority position offer acceptable 
risk-adjusted returns given our 3.0x MOIC / 25% IRR target?
```

**Answer (Pyramid Top)**:
```
INVESTMENT DECISION: Proceed with $200M investment for 40% stake.

Expected returns: 3.2x MOIC, 27% IRR over 5 years—above our hurdle 
despite structural constraints.
```

**Supporting Arguments (Pyramid Second Level)**:
```
1. Exceptional unit economics justify premium entry multiple
   1.1 CAC payback: 8 months (sector median: 18 months)
   1.2 Net revenue retention: 125% (top decile)
   1.3 LTV/CAC ratio: 6.2x (sustainable competitive advantage)

2. Multiple expansion thesis has three drivers
   2.1 Margin trajectory: 15% → 30% at scale (proven in comps)
   2.2 Market leadership in growing TAM ($8B → $25B by 2028)
   2.3 Entry at 20x on current EBITDA = 14x on normalized margins

3. Downside protection despite minority position
   3.1 Founder economically aligned (retains 60%, no liquidity)
   3.2 Strategic buyers active in space (4 acquisitions at 25x+ in 2023)
   3.3 Board seat + veto rights on major decisions
```

### Ordering Ideas: Top-Down vs. Bottom-Up

#### Top-Down (Start with Answer - Recommended)

**When to use**: When you know your conclusion.

**Process**:
```
1. State your main point (Answer from SCQA)
2. Identify the question it raises
3. Group your supporting ideas (3-5 reasons)
4. Gather evidence for each group
```

**Example (Valuation Opinion)**:
```
1. Main point: "Fair value is $450M (DCF midpoint)"
2. Question it raises: "How did you get to $450M?"
3. Supporting groups:
   • DCF analysis: $420M - $480M range
   • Market multiples: $400M - $500M range  
   • Precedent transactions: $430M - $470M range
4. Evidence: Detailed calculations for each method
```

#### Bottom-Up (Build from Data - When Exploring)

**When to use**: When you don't yet know your conclusion.

**Process**:
```
1. List all relevant facts/data
2. Group related items together
3. Identify what each group proves
4. Determine overall conclusion
```

**Example (Problem Diagnosis)**:
```
1. Facts observed:
   • Gross margin declining 300bps over 3 years
   • Unit prices stable (no pricing pressure)
   • COGS/unit increasing 15% annually
   • Supplier concentration: top 3 = 70% of COGS
   • Industry raw material costs up 8% (company up 15%)
   
2. Grouping:
   All facts relate to supplier costs rising faster than market
   
3. What it proves:
   Weak supplier negotiation / unfavorable contracts
   
4. Conclusion:
   Margin pressure driven by supplier-side issues, not market pricing
```

### Common Mistakes in Financial Writing

#### Mistake 1: Starting with Background Instead of SCQA

**❌ Weak Opening**:
```
"ABC Company was founded in 1995 and went public in 2010. 
The company operates in three segments: North America, Europe, 
and Asia. Revenues have grown from $500M to $2B over the past 
decade. The company has 5,000 employees..."
```

Reader reaction: *Get to the point. Why am I reading this?*

**✅ Strong Opening (SCQA)**:
```
"We recommend selling ABC Company stock (Price Target: $25, 
-30% downside). Despite revenue growth, deteriorating returns on 
capital and unsustainable leverage position the company for a 
significant multiple de-rating."
```

#### Mistake 2: Violating Vertical Logic (Not Answering the Question)

**❌ Broken Logic**:
```
Recommendation: "Invest in Company X"
    ↓ (Why?)
Support: "The management team has 20 years of experience"
```

This doesn't answer "why invest." It answers "is management capable?"

**✅ Proper Logic**:
```
Recommendation: "Invest in Company X"  
    ↓ (Why?)
Support: "Expected 3.5x MOIC over 5 years exceeds our 3.0x hurdle"
    ↓ (How will you achieve 3.5x?)
Evidence:
    • Entry valuation: 8x EBITDA (peer average: 11x)
    • EBITDA growth: 15% CAGR (operational improvements)
    • Exit multiple: 10x (quality improvements justify re-rating)
    • Math: 8x → 10x + 2x growth = 3.5x MOIC
```

#### Mistake 3: Violating Horizontal Logic (Mixing Idea Types)

**❌ Mixed Logic**:
```
Three reasons to divest:
1. ROIC below WACC (financial metric)
2. Division doesn't fit strategy (strategic consideration)
3. Buyer paying premium (transaction circumstance)
```

These aren't the same kind of reason.

**✅ Consistent Logic (All Financial)**:
```
Divest based on three financial indicators:
1. Negative economic profit: ROIC 5% < WACC 9%
2. Declining returns: ROIC down from 12% three years ago
3. Below market value: Offer at 1.5x book (division carrying value: 1.0x)
```

**Or Consistent Logic (All Strategic)**:
```
Divest for strategic portfolio reasons:
1. Non-core: Outside our technology focus
2. Unfixable position: Structurally disadvantaged vs. competitors
3. Capital redeployment: Proceeds fund core business expansion
```

### Document Types & Pyramid Application

| **Document Type** | **Pyramid Top (Answer)** | **SCQA Complication** | **Key Supporting Groups** |
|------------------|------------------------|---------------------|------------------------|
| **Investment Memo** | Buy/Sell/Hold + Target Price | Stock mispricing vs. fundamentals | Financial / Valuation / Catalysts |
| **Valuation Report** | Fair value conclusion | Need for independent valuation | DCF / Multiples / Sensitivity |
| **Board Decision Paper** | Recommended action | Strategic choice required | Strategic / Financial / Execution |
| **Credit Memo** | Lend / Don't Lend + Terms | Borrower requesting credit | Cash Flow / Collateral / Covenants |
| **M&A Recommendation** | Acquire / Pass + Price | Target available for sale | Strategic Fit / Returns / Risks |

### Quality Checklist for Pyramid Structure

Before finalizing any financial document, verify:

**✅ SCQA Present**:
- [ ] Situation: Uncontroversial facts establishing context
- [ ] Complication: Problem/opportunity creating tension
- [ ] Question: What reader wants answered
- [ ] Answer: Your clear recommendation/conclusion

**✅ Vertical Logic (Question/Answer)**:
- [ ] Each level answers question from above
- [ ] Main point triggers "why?" or "how?"
- [ ] Supporting points answer that question
- [ ] Evidence proves supporting points

**✅ Horizontal Logic (Grouping)**:
- [ ] Ideas at same level are same TYPE
- [ ] Deductive = logical argument (if X then Y)
- [ ] Inductive = similar observations → pattern
- [ ] 3-5 groups at each level (not 10+)

**✅ Reader Experience**:
- [ ] Main point stated first (not buried)
- [ ] No need to re-read for comprehension
- [ ] Structure visible (headings reflect pyramid)
- [ ] Each section answers implied question

### Integration with Valuation Work

**When writing a DCF valuation report**:

```
Pyramid Structure:

Fair Value: $450M (DCF midpoint, $8.50/share)
├── Revenue Projections
│   ├── Historical CAGR: 12% (2019-2023)
│   ├── Forecast CAGR: 10% (market growth normalization)
│   └── Key driver: Customer acquisition at 500/year
│
├── Margin Assumptions
│   ├── Gross margin: 65% (current 60%, efficiency gains)
│   ├── EBITDA margin: 22% (current 18%, scale benefits)
│   └── Path validated by comparable analysis
│
├── Terminal Value
│   ├── Perpetuity growth: 3% (GDP + inflation)
│   ├── Terminal EBITDA multiple: 10x (peer median)
│   └── Cross-check: Perpetuity → 9.8x implied multiple
│
└── WACC: 9.2%
    ├── Cost of equity: 11.5% (CAPM)
    ├── After-tax cost of debt: 4.2%
    └── Capital structure: 70/30 equity/debt
```

Each section answers the question: "How did you arrive at this assumption?"
