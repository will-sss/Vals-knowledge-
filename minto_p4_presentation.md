# Minto Pyramid Principle - Logic in Presentation

## Part 4: Reflecting the Pyramid in Documents and Slides

### Core Concept

The pyramid structure must be visible to the reader, not just present in your mind. Whether writing prose or creating presentation slides, use structural signals (headings, transitions, formatting) to make the logical relationships explicit. Financial documents that hide their structure force readers to reconstruct the logic themselves—wasting time and risking misunderstanding. Clear presentation ensures your valuation logic, investment recommendation, or strategic analysis is immediately comprehensible.

### Reflecting the Pyramid on the Page (Written Documents)

#### Technique 1: Hierarchical Headings

**Purpose**: Headings telegraph the pyramid structure and help readers navigate.

**Financial Document Example - Valuation Report**:

```
VALUATION OPINION: Company X
Fair Value: $450 Million (Range: $400M - $500M)

1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
   Recommendation and Key Drivers

2. COMPANY OVERVIEW
   Business Model and Market Position
   
3. VALUATION METHODOLOGY
   
   3.1 Discounted Cash Flow Analysis
       3.1.1 Revenue Projections
       3.1.2 Margin Assumptions
       3.1.3 Terminal Value Calculation
       3.1.4 WACC Determination
       
   3.2 Market Approach
       3.2.1 Trading Comparables
       3.2.2 Precedent Transactions
       
   3.3 Reconciliation of Methods

4. SENSITIVITY ANALYSIS
   Key Value Drivers and Scenario Testing

5. CONCLUSION AND LIMITATIONS
```

**Rules**:
- Main headings (1, 2, 3) = Pyramid second level
- Subheadings (3.1, 3.2) = Pyramid third level
- Sub-subheadings (3.1.1) = Supporting evidence

**Test**: Can reader understand structure from headings alone? ✓

#### Technique 2: Underlined Topic Sentences

**Purpose**: Signal the main point of each section before explaining.

**Example - Investment Memo Section**:

```
FINANCIAL PERFORMANCE SUPPORTS PREMIUM VALUATION

The company has demonstrated consistent above-market growth with 
expanding margins. Revenue has grown at 15% CAGR over three years 
(sector: 8%), while EBITDA margins expanded from 18% to 22%. This 
trajectory reflects both market share gains and operational leverage.

[Supporting details and data tables follow...]
```

**Not**:
```
Financial Performance

Revenue for the past three years was $800M, $920M, and $1,058M...
[Reader must work to figure out what this proves]
```

**Rule**: First sentence of each section = summary of that pyramid branch.

#### Technique 3: Transition Statements

**Purpose**: Show logical relationships between sections.

**Types of Transitions**:

**1. Moving Down the Pyramid** (from summary to detail):
```
"This conclusion rests on three key assumptions..."
"Two factors drive this valuation range..."
"The recommendation is supported by the following analysis..."
```

**2. Moving Sideways** (between parallel ideas):
```
"In addition to strategic fit, financial returns are compelling..."
"Beyond the quantitative analysis, qualitative factors reinforce..."
"The second component of enterprise value is terminal value..."
```

**3. Moving Up** (from detail to summary):
```
"These three findings collectively indicate..."
"Taken together, these metrics suggest..."
"This analysis therefore supports the conclusion that..."
```

**Example - Connecting Sections**:
```
VALUATION METHODS CONVERGE ON $450M FAIR VALUE

Three independent methods support this conclusion. [↓ moving down]

First, the DCF analysis yields $440M... [detail]

Second, trading comparables suggest $460M... [→ sideways, parallel method]

Third, precedent transactions indicate $450M... [→ sideways, third method]

All three methods converge around $450M, with sensitivity 
driven primarily by terminal value assumptions. [↑ moving up to summary]
```

#### Technique 4: Numbered/Bulleted Lists (Use Sparingly)

**When to use**: For parallel items at same pyramid level.

**Investment Memo Example**:

```
Three catalysts could drive near-term re-rating:

1. Product launch (Q2 2024): New SaaS module adds $50M revenue
   • Currently in beta with 5 enterprise customers
   • 40% gross margin (above company average of 35%)
   
2. Margin expansion initiative (FY 2024): Targets +300bps EBITDA margin
   • Procurement savings: +100bps
   • Headcount efficiency: +200bps
   
3. Strategic acquisition (H2 2024): Management indicated M&A appetite
   • $200M cash available for acquisitions
   • Targeting adjacent product categories
```

**Rules**:
- Only use when items are truly parallel (same level)
- Each item should be 1-3 sentences (not paragraphs)
- Maintain consistent grammatical structure

**Avoid** for main document flow—save for supporting detail.

### Reflecting the Pyramid in Presentations (Slides)

#### Principle 1: One Idea Per Slide

**Each slide = one box in your pyramid**

**❌ Wrong - Multiple Ideas**:
```
Slide Title: "Financial Analysis"

• Revenue growing 15%
• Margins expanding 200bps
• Market share stable at 22%
• Competitor launched new product
• Regulatory environment changing
• Customer satisfaction improving
```

Overwhelming. What's the point?

**✅ Right - Single Message**:
```
Slide Title: "Strong Financial Momentum Supports Valuation Expansion"

Revenue Growth: 15% CAGR (vs. sector 8%)
Margin Expansion: 18% → 22% EBITDA margin
Scale Benefits: Fixed cost leverage materializing

[Chart showing revenue and margin trends]

Implication: Justifies 12x EV/EBITDA (peer median: 10x)
```

**Rule**: Slide title = pyramid point. Slide content = supporting evidence.

#### Principle 2: Slide Titles Are Action Statements

**Not labels, but conclusions**

**❌ Descriptive Labels**:
```
• "Market Analysis"
• "Comparable Companies"
• "DCF Assumptions"
• "Risk Factors"
```

Reader learns nothing from titles alone.

**✅ Action Statements (Pyramid Points)**:
```
• "Addressable market growing 12% annually with low penetration"
• "Target trades at 30% discount to comparable companies"
• "Conservative DCF assumptions support $450M valuation"
• "Three mitigable risks identified in due diligence"
```

Reader can follow logic from titles alone.

**Investment Committee Presentation Example**:

```
Slide 1: "Recommend acquiring TechCo for $200M (3.2x MOIC, 27% IRR)"

Slide 2: "Strong unit economics justify premium entry multiple"
   [CAC payback, LTV/CAC, NRR data]

Slide 3: "Multiple expansion thesis supported by three drivers"
   [Margin trajectory, market leadership, valuation gap]

Slide 4: "Downside protected despite minority position"
   [Governance rights, strategic buyer interest, founder alignment]

Slide 5: "Three execution risks require active monitoring"
   [Customer concentration, key employee retention, integration complexity]
```

**Test**: Can I understand recommendation by reading titles only? ✓

#### Principle 3: Storyboarding Before Slide Creation

**Process**:
1. Build pyramid structure first (on paper)
2. Convert each pyramid box to slide title
3. Check flow: Does title sequence tell complete story?
4. Only then create actual slides

**Example - M&A Recommendation Storyboard**:

```
Pyramid Structure:
┌─────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Acquire Company X ($500M, 3.5x MOIC)    │
└─────────────┬───────────────────────────┘
              │
    ┌─────────┴─────────┬──────────────┐
    │                   │              │
Strategic Fit    Financial Returns   Executable
    │                   │              │
3 slides         4 slides         3 slides
```

**Slide Titles (Following Pyramid)**:

```
1. RECOMMENDATION: Acquire Company X for $500M

STRATEGIC FIT:
2. Acquisition expands TAM 3x into adjacent $2B market
3. Eliminates key competitor and consolidates market position
4. Creates platform for international expansion

FINANCIAL RETURNS:
5. Base case IRR of 28% exceeds 25% hurdle
6. Revenue synergies of $50M (cross-sell to combined base)
7. Cost synergies of $30M (eliminate duplicative functions)
8. Multiple arbitrage: buy at 8x, exit at 11x (peer median)

EXECUTABLE:
9. Management team committed to 3-year earnout
10. Integration risk low (complementary products, minimal overlap)
11. Financing secured: $300M debt + $200M equity approved
```

**Test**: Reading titles 1-11 in sequence = complete argument ✓

#### Principle 4: Visual Hierarchy on Slides

**Use formatting to show pyramid levels**:

**Example Slide Layout**:

```
┌───────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ STRONG UNIT ECONOMICS JUSTIFY PREMIUM VALUATION       │  ← Main point (bold, large)
├───────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│                                                       │
│ Three metrics demonstrate best-in-class efficiency:   │  ← Setup (normal text)
│                                                       │
│ • CAC Payback: 8 months (peer avg: 18 months)       │  ← Evidence level 1
│   - Sales efficiency 2.2x peer average               │     (indent = sub-point)
│   - Organic acquisition (50% of new logos)           │
│                                                       │
│ • LTV/CAC Ratio: 6.2x (peer median: 3.5x)           │  ← Evidence level 2
│   - High NRR (125%) drives lifetime value           │
│   - Low churn (5% vs peer 15%)                      │
│                                                       │
│ • Gross Margin: 85% (peer range: 65-75%)            │  ← Evidence level 3
│   - Software-only (no services drag)                 │
│   - Efficient cloud infrastructure                   │
│                                                       │
│ Implication: Warrants 20x revenue multiple          │  ← Conclusion (italics)
│ (2x premium to 10x peer median justified)            │
└───────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**Visual Cues**:
- **Bold large font**: Main message (pyramid point)
- **Bullets**: Supporting evidence (next pyramid level)
- **Indented sub-bullets**: Detailed proof
- **Italics at bottom**: "So what" implication

#### Principle 5: Exhibits Support, Don't Replace, Slide Message

**❌ Chart Without Context**:
```
Slide Title: "Revenue Trends"

[Line chart showing revenue 2020-2023]
```

Reader must interpret chart themselves. What should they conclude?

**✅ Chart With Interpretation**:
```
Slide Title: "Revenue Growth Accelerating Above Market"

[Line chart showing:]
• Company revenue: $800M → $1.2B (15% CAGR)
• Market growth: 8% CAGR (shown as reference line)
• Market share: 18% → 22% (gaining share)

Key insight: Outperformance driven by new product adoption
(new products = 60% of growth vs. 30% in 2020)
```

**Rule**: Chart reinforces slide title, never replaces it.

### Reflecting the Pyramid in Prose

#### Opening Paragraph = Executive Summary

**Investment Memo Example**:

```
RECOMMENDATION: BUY TechCo (Target: $55, 57% upside)

Market is mispricing durable competitive advantages and 
underestimating margin expansion potential. Despite 30% revenue 
growth and 85% gross margins, the stock has underperformed 
peers by 40% over 12 months. Three factors support our price 
target: (1) best-in-class unit economics justify premium 
valuation, (2) margin expansion path to 30% EBITDA (currently 
15%), and (3) multiple re-rating likely as quality metrics 
improve. Downside limited to $40 (15% from current) given 
strategic buyer interest and $10/share cash position.
```

**This paragraph**:
- States conclusion (top of pyramid)
- Lists 3 main supporting arguments (second level)
- Mentions key evidence (third level: unit economics, margins, multiples)
- Addresses risks (downside case)

**Rule**: Reader should understand full recommendation from paragraph one.

#### Paragraph Structure Mirrors Pyramid

**Each paragraph = one pyramid box**

**Structure**:
```
[Topic sentence: Main point of this pyramid box]
[Supporting sentence 1: Evidence]
[Supporting sentence 2: Evidence]
[Supporting sentence 3: Evidence]
[Concluding sentence: Implication/transition]
```

**Example - Paragraph on Competitive Advantages**:

```
TechCo's competitive moat rests on proprietary data and network 
effects. [main point] The platform has accumulated 10 years of 
customer usage data, creating ML-driven insights competitors 
cannot replicate without similar scale. [evidence 1] Network 
effects strengthen as customer count grows (current: 5,000 
enterprises), making the platform more valuable to each 
participant. [evidence 2] Switching costs are high, evidenced 
by 95% annual retention and 12-month implementation cycles for 
competing products. [evidence 3] These advantages should sustain 
pricing power and market share, supporting margin expansion to 
30% from current 15%. [implication]
```

#### Section Summaries and Transitions

**At end of major sections**: Summarize what was proven

**Example - End of Financial Analysis Section**:

```
In summary, TechCo's financial performance demonstrates three 
characteristics that justify a premium valuation: accelerating 
revenue growth (15% → 20% → 25% trajectory), improving capital 
efficiency (ROIC expanding from 12% to 18%), and strong cash 
generation (FCF/revenue improving to 20%). These trends support 
our forecast of 30% EBITDA margins within three years, validating 
a 15x EV/EBITDA multiple (vs. current 12x).

Having established the financial foundation, we turn next to 
competitive positioning to assess sustainability of these trends. 
[transition to next section]
```

### Common Presentation Mistakes

#### Mistake 1: Pyramid Structure Invisible

**❌ No Structural Signals**:
```
[Wall of text with no headings, bullets, or formatting]

The company operates in three segments. Revenue last year was 
$1B. Margins are 15%. The market is growing 8%. Competition is 
increasing. Management is experienced. The balance sheet is 
strong. We recommend investing...
```

Reader must reconstruct logic themselves.

**✅ Structure Visible**:
```
RECOMMENDATION: Invest in Company X (3.5x MOIC opportunity)

Three factors support this conclusion:

1. STRONG FINANCIAL MOMENTUM
   Revenue: $1B (+15% vs. market +8%)
   Margins: Expanding to 18% from 15%
   ROIC: 18% vs. WACC 9% (value creation)

2. COMPETITIVE ADVANTAGES SUSTAINABLE
   Market position: #2 with 22% share (growing)
   Customer retention: 95% (high switching costs)
   Management: 15+ years experience, proven track record

3. VALUATION ATTRACTIVE
   Current: 8x EV/EBITDA (peer median: 11x)
   Implied: 12x on normalized margins
   Return potential: 3.5x MOIC over 5 years
```

#### Mistake 2: Slide Titles Don't Tell Story

**❌ Generic Labels**:
```
Slide 1: Introduction
Slide 2: Market Overview
Slide 3: Financial Analysis
Slide 4: Valuation
Slide 5: Conclusion
```

Titles don't communicate thinking.

**✅ Action Statements**:
```
Slide 1: Acquire TechCo for $200M (27% IRR, 3.2x MOIC)
Slide 2: $8B market growing 12% with 15% penetration
Slide 3: Strong unit economics justify premium entry multiple
Slide 4: Conservative assumptions support $200M valuation
Slide 5: Three risks mitigated through deal structure
```

#### Mistake 3: Burying the Lead

**❌ Background First**:
```
Company X was founded in 1995. It went public in 2010. 
Revenue has grown from $500M to $2B. The company operates 
in North America and Europe. Management has 20 years 
experience. After analyzing all this...we recommend selling.
```

Reader waits too long for the point.

**✅ Conclusion First**:
```
RECOMMENDATION: SELL Company X (Price Target: $25, -30% downside)

Despite historical growth, deteriorating returns and 
unsustainable leverage position the company for significant 
multiple de-rating. Three factors drive our bearish view:
[then explain background as supporting evidence]
```

### Integration: Document + Presentation Alignment

**For major decisions (M&A, large investments)**, create aligned package:

**Written Memo** (Detailed Analysis):
```
30-page investment memo with full DCF model, 
sensitivity analysis, detailed due diligence findings
```

**Presentation** (Board Meeting):
```
15 slides extracting pyramid structure from memo:
- Same conclusion
- Same 3 main arguments
- Same key evidence
- Condensed for 30-minute presentation
```

**Alignment Check**:
- Memo executive summary = Slide 1
- Memo section headers = Slide titles
- Memo appendices = Backup slides

**Test**: Can board approve based on presentation, then reference memo for detail? ✓

### Quality Checklist for Presentation

Before finalizing document or slides:

**✅ Structure Visible**:
- [ ] Can reader understand logic from headings alone?
- [ ] Are pyramid levels clearly distinguished?
- [ ] Are transitions explicit between sections?

**✅ Message Clear**:
- [ ] Is conclusion stated in first paragraph/slide?
- [ ] Does each section have clear topic sentence/title?
- [ ] Is "so what" obvious for each point?

**✅ Evidence Organized**:
- [ ] Is supporting data grouped logically?
- [ ] Are charts/tables properly interpreted?
- [ ] Is level of detail appropriate for audience?

**✅ Flow Natural**:
- [ ] Does argument build logically?
- [ ] Are questions answered when they arise?
- [ ] Can reader follow without re-reading?
